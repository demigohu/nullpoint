## What the vault is worth

**NAV** (net asset value) is everything the vault owns minus what it owes. **Share price** = NAV ÷ all shares. Deposits and withdrawals are priced off it, so it has to be hard to fake.

| Part | Source | Can the agent fake it? |
|---|---|---|
| Idle USDC | Read by the contract from `intents.near` | No |
| Coin held | Balance read by the contract from `intents.near` | No |
| Coin price | 1Click quotes verified by the contract | Only by choosing among real quotes; they carry 1Click's fee, so the error leans low |
| Hyperliquid account | Reported by the agent | Within the drift check below |

## The report check

The contract can't read Hyperliquid, so the agent reports the account value and the shorts. The contract compares that with what it expects:

**expected = last accepted value + collateral moved since + change in value of the open short from the price moves**

A report is accepted when it's within `max_report_drift_bps` (2%) of the largest of the short's value, the old value and the expected value, plus a dust allowance (0.5 USDC testnet, 1.5 USDC mainnet). Collateral sent to Hyperliquid counts at the amount 1Click quoted to deliver.

If the gap is bigger, the contract **pauses the vault**: no deposits, withdrawals or swaps until the owner investigates. Funding and price moves fit inside the check; a stolen key reporting a made-up number doesn't.

A report built on a price older than 120 seconds is simply **dropped**, not treated as a violation. The old NAV stays, and the agent reports again next run.

## The record

The contract keeps a history of `(time, NAV, shares)`, about 500 entries, written at most every 10 minutes on testnet and every 30 on mainnet. The dashboard charts the share price from it. A gap in that history means the agent stopped reporting, which makes it a cheap uptime record too.

## Fees in the NAV

The performance fee is taken inside the report: if the new share price is above the high-water mark, the contract mints 5% of the gain to the treasury as shares before it records the new price. See [Fees](/fees).
